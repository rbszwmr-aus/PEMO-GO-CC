# GEN-009 — The scope-only write skeleton

The authority freeze is lifted and the PO chose the 27 scope-only writes (`ADR-010` `D1`) as the first product track. This is the first of them, deliberately thin, to establish and prove `D1`'s four audit properties **once** so the other 26 reuse the pattern rather than re-deriving it.

## What to build

The **audited scope-only write path**, with a single case operation — adding a case note, or the nearest non-escalation case action the current model already carries — as its first and only consumer. The reusable thing is the path and its guarantees; the case note is the vehicle that proves them.

Two halves, both required by `D1`:

1. **The scope gate.** The act is authorized through `(group, role, node)` — the caller must hold a grant at the case's node. Out-of-scope is **absent**, not refused-after-fetch: the target the caller has no scope for does not resolve for them, the same filter-in-query shape reads already use. `estate_staff` at the node is the grant that authorizes it.
2. **The audit record, carrying all four `D1` properties**, written **in the same transaction as the act**: attributable to a **principal**, never a session, service or job; **effective-dated**, answerable against the grant in force at that time; and **not deletable or amendable by the actor**.

## Step 1 — assess before building

Do not assume the machinery is greenfield. `ADR-010` `D1` names the **resident acknowledgement** as the one write this project has already shipped. Read that path. Report which of the four audit properties it already carries and whether the write path generalises from it or must be built. Propose the case-note contract (endpoint, request/response, the audit-record shape) and the exact files. **HARD STOP** — the Brain rules the contract and the reuse-or-build call before any implementation.

## Done when

Each proven by making it fail, not asserted.

**The scope gate:** a principal holding `estate_staff` at the case's node performs the act and it persists; remove the grant and the identical act does not resolve the target and does not persist. Scope is the gate, and out-of-scope is absent.

**The four audit properties, each with its red shown:**

1. **Same transaction** — inject a failure in the audit write; the **act rolls back**. There is no state in which the act persisted and the audit did not.
2. **Attributable** — an act with no attributable principal is rejected; the audit cannot be written against a session or service identity.
3. **Effective-dated** — "what could this principal do at time T" resolves against the grant in force at T; a membership dated `now()` returns nobody for a past act, and the effective date makes it answerable (the silent-empty failure `GEN-006`'s §D red exposed — show it bites here too).
4. **Immutable to the actor** — the actor attempting to update or delete their own audit row is refused, **at the level that makes it a guarantee and not a convention** (DB permission, not application courtesy). Show the refusal.

## Why thin, and why these four are non-negotiable

`D1` released 27 writes on scope alone **on the condition** that the audit record carries the weight the gate would have: *without all four, record-rather-than-restrict records nothing.* The skeleton's value is not the case note — it is proving the four properties hold on a real write, once, so every later scope-only write inherits a path that is already proven. The 26 that follow are cheap **only** because this one was not.
