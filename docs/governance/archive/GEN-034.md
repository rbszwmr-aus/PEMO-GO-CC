# GEN-034 — R5: governance conventions, from memory to enforcement

**A rule whose membership is grepped by hand — or whose scan silently ignores what falls outside its
pattern — is a suggestion, not a rule.**

Six conventions this corpus learned by being refused: the `pids/` stem the allocator cannot see, the
`-RULINGS-` name discovered only at a close, the surface enum regenerated from memory, mutation by
deletion rather than by a competing rule, the bracketed token that means three different things, and
one thin response echo.

## OWED — elevated to R5b, first-class

**The validator-code derivation is itself fragile.** It greps `fail('…')` call sites out of
`validate.mjs` for its 73 codes, so a change to that call shape **silently narrows** the set, and the
vacuity refusal catches only a total failure, not a partial one. **The same "derived axis, fragile
derivation" shape this PID retires, one level down.**

**It does not block anything, and the reason is the failure direction:** a narrowed set misreads a
bracketed code as a dangling principle, `adrs-present` fails, and a close is refused. **A false
positive that fails loud and masks nothing.**

**The retirement:** give validator codes a **declared** source of truth — an enum or union that
`validate.mjs` consumes — and have the citation derivation read *that*, not the call sites.
Symmetric with the Problem.type axis, which already derives from a declaration.

**R5b also still holds:** the `LIKE`-escape sweep, fixtures `ON CONFLICT`,
derive-refusing-commands-from-types, and `with-mutation.sh`.
